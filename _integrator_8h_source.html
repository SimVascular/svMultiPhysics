12/**
13 * @brief Integrator class that encapsulates the Newton iteration loop for time
14 * integration.
15 *
16 * This class handles the nonlinear Newton iteration scheme for solving coupled
17 * multi-physics equations in svMultiPhysics. It manages:
18 * - Solution variables (Ag, Yg, Dg) at generalized-alpha time levels
19 * - Newton iteration loop with convergence checking
20 * - Linear system assembly and solve
21 * - Boundary condition application
22 *
23 * ## Line search
24 *
25 * The nonlinear iterations of an equation can be globalized by a backtracking
26 * line search, enabled by the `Line_search_experimental` XML element of the
27 * equation (see @ref LineSearchParameters). Without it, every iteration applies
28 * the full increment of the linear solve.
29 *
30 * At every nonlinear iteration of an equation with line search enabled, the
31 * increment @f$\delta u@f$ of the linear solve is scaled by a step length
32 * @f$\alpha@f$ before being applied, so that the solution is updated to
33 * @f$u + \alpha\,\delta u@f$. The step lengths @f$\alpha = 1, \rho, \rho^2,
34 * \dots@f$ are tried in turn, with @f$\rho@f$ the step reduction factor, and
35 * the first one satisfying
36 * @f[
37 * \| R(u + \alpha\,\delta u) \|_W \leq (1 - c\,\alpha)\,\| R(u) \|_W
38 * @f]
39 * is applied, with @f$c@f$ the minimum decrease. Once the step length reaches
40 * the minimum step length, it is applied regardless of the residual decrease.
41 *
42 * The norm @f$\|\cdot\|_W@f$ weights every residual entry by the inverse
43 * square root of the magnitude of the corresponding diagonal entry of the
44 * tangent at @f$u@f$, as the Jacobi preconditioner of the linear solver does,
45 * so that it is dimensionally homogeneous across the degrees of freedom of the
46 * equation. Degrees of freedom constrained by a Dirichlet condition have zero
47 * weight, since their residual is the reaction of the constraint. The weights
48 * are computed once per iteration and kept fixed while step lengths are tried,
49 * so that the norms of different step lengths are comparable (see
50 * compute_residual_weights).
51 *
52 * The linear system is solved once per iteration, whatever the number of step
53 * lengths tried. Measuring a step length costs one assembly of the linear
54 * system at the trial solution, which re-advances implicitly coupled active
55 * stress models as any other assembly does. The convergence test that follows
56 * (see finalize_iteration) is the same with or without line search.
57 *
58 * Line search is not implemented for prestress simulations: the nodal
59 * prestress is accumulated over the assemblies of a time step, which would
60 * include those of the trial step lengths unless they were excluded.
61 *
62 * @warning While it is implemented for all equations, line search is currently
63 * only tested for struct, should be regarded as experimental, and used with
64 * caution.
65 */

198 /**
199 * @brief Search for a step length that decreases the residual.
200 *
201 * Measures the increment of the linear solve at decreasing step lengths,
202 * starting from one, until the residual assembled at the resulting solution
203 * falls below \p reference_norm by the margin the settings of the equation
204 * ask for, or until the shortest step length is reached, which is returned
205 * without being measured. Measuring a step length costs one assembly of the
206 * linear system, while the increment itself is reused, so the linear system
207 * is solved once per nonlinear iteration however many step lengths are
208 * tried.
209 *
210 * The solution and the increment are left as they were found, so that the
211 * caller can apply the returned step length to them.
212 *
213 * @param[in,out] eq Equation whose increment is measured, and whose settings
214 * configure the search.
215 * @param[in] weights Weight of every residual entry in the norm, as computed
216 * by compute_residual_weights at the solution the search starts from.
217 * @param[in] reference_norm Norm of the residual at the solution the search
218 * starts from, measured with \p weights.
219 *
220 * @return Step length to apply to the increment.
221 */

246 /**
247 * @brief Compute the weight of every residual entry in the residual norm.
248 *
249 * The weight of a degree of freedom is the inverse square root of the
250 * magnitude of the corresponding diagonal entry of the tangent, assembled
251 * across processes, which is what the Jacobi preconditioner of the linear
252 * solver scales the rows of the linear system by. The weight of a degree of
253 * freedom constrained by a Dirichlet condition is zero, so that the residual
254 * norm ignores the rows the linear solve eliminates, whose residual is a
255 * reaction rather than an equation left to be satisfied.
256 *
257 * The resulting norm is dimensionally homogeneous across the degrees of
258 * freedom of an equation, which a norm of the residual entries themselves is
259 * not.
260 *
261 * The weights are read from the tangent assembled last, which the assembly
262 * of a further solution overwrites, so a line search computes them once at
263 * the solution it starts from and reuses them for every step length it
264 * tries. Keeping them fixed is what makes the norm a function of the
265 * solution alone, and so makes the norms of two step lengths comparable.
266 *
267 * @param[out] weights Weight of every degree of freedom of every node, in
268 * the node numbering of the linear solver.
269 */
270 void compute_residual_weights(Array<double> &weights) const;
271
272 /**
273 * @brief Compute the weighted norm of the assembled residual
274 *
275 * Sums the squares of the weighted entries of com_mod.R belonging to the
276 * nodes owned by each process and reduces them across processes, so that the
277 * result does not depend on the partitioning. It is available as soon as the
278 * residual has been assembled, before the linear system is solved.
279 *
280 * com_mod.R holds the residual of the equation assembled last, and its rows
281 * are the degrees of freedom of that equation, numbered from zero.
282 *
283 * @param[in] weights Weight of every degree of freedom of every node, as
284 * computed by compute_residual_weights.
285 *
286 * @return Weighted Euclidean norm of the assembled residual.
287 */
288 double residual_norm(const Array<double> &weights) const;

347 /**
348 * @brief Apply a multiple of the increment of the linear solve to the
349 * solution
350 *
351 * Advances the solution of an equation at the n+1 time level by @c
352 * step_length times the increment held in com_mod.R (and com_mod.Rd for the
353 * velocity-based formulation), and updates the quantities that are derived
354 * from the resulting solution: the Taylor-Hood pressure at edge nodes, the
355 * copy of the solution onto the solid subdomain of an FSI equation, the
356 * ionic state of an electrophysiology equation, and the wall filter of a CMM
357 * equation.
358 *
359 * The solution change is proportional to step_length, so calling this
360 * function from the same starting solution with different step lengths traces
361 * the segment between that solution and the one the full increment produces.
362 * Every quantity derived from the solution is recomputed, so any step length
363 * leaves a consistent state.
364 *
365 * Modifies:
366 * \code {.cpp}
367 * com_mod.Ad
368 * solutions_.current.A
369 * solutions_.current.D
370 * solutions_.current.Y
371 * cep_mod.Xion
372 * \endcode
373 *
374 * @param[in] eq Equation whose solution is advanced, which selects the
375 * degrees of freedom the increment is applied to and the coefficients of
376 * the time integration scheme.
377 * @param[in] step_length Step length multiplying the increment. One applies
378 * the full increment of the linear solve.
379 */
380 void apply_increment(const eqType &eq, const double step_length);

397 * com_mod.pSn
398 * solutions_.current.A
399 * solutions_.current.D
400 * solutions_.current.Y
