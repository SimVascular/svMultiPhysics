Detailed Description

Prestrain of a body imaged while subject to a load.

The mesh is the imaged geometry, which is subject to a load. The prestrain is a deformation gradient \( F_0 \) at every Gauss point that maps an unknown stress-free configuration to the mesh. A displacement \( u \) from the mesh then gives the deformation gradient from the stress-free configuration by the multiplicative decomposition

\[ F = (I + \nabla u) \, F_0, \]

with \( \nabla \) the gradient on the mesh.

The prestrain is used in a structural simulation to pull the integrals of the weak form back to the stress-free configuration. The solid kernels (struct_ns::struct_2d and struct_3d) transform

  • the shape function gradients, \( \nabla N \to \nabla_0 N = F_0^T \nabla N \),
  • the volume element, \( dV \to dV / \det F_0 \),
  • every fiber direction, \( f \to f_0 = F_0^{-1} f / |F_0^{-1} f| \).

The solid kernels then keep their total Lagrangian form, with the deformation gradient above, computed from the displacement on the mesh as \( F = (I + \sum_a u_a \otimes \nabla N_a) \, F_0 \).

A fiber is a material line, so \( F_0^{-1} \) carries it from the mesh to the stress-free configuration and changes its length by the stretch the prestrain gives it. The material laws expect a unit direction in their reference configuration and measure the fiber stretch as \( |F f_0| \), so the pulled-back vector is normalized; at \( u = 0 \) that stretch is then the fiber's prestretch, \( 1 / |F_0^{-1} f| \). Every fiber is pulled back on its own, so a basis orthonormal on the mesh is orthonormal in the imaged configuration, not in the stress-free one.

The formulation is objective: rotating the deformed body rigidly rotates \( F \) and the internal forces with it.

\( F_0 \) is found by Newton's method on the imaged geometry. Every Newton iteration starts from rest under the full load, so its residual is that of the current \( F_0 \). The displacement \( u \) it reaches is composed into the prestrain,

\[ F_0 \leftarrow (I + \nabla u) \, F_0, \]

and discarded. At the fixed point the mesh carries the load in Cauchy stress equilibrium without moving. This is the incremental scheme of Gee, Forster and Wall (2010), section 3, with the increments composed on the imaged mesh, so that equilibrium holds on the imaged geometry rather than on a virtually deformed one. Because each iteration is composed, the tangent of the composed residual differs from the kernel's own, and the solid kernels correct it (see struct_ns::prestrain_tangent_correction). Max_iterations and Tolerance bound the iterations of a time step as in any other run. The residual force of every step is written to prestrain_residual.csv in the results folder, and with Adaptive_time_step the time step is adapted to the residual between steps (see Integrator.h).

Each mesh holds \( F_0 \) in a Prestrain, one tensor per Gauss point, written to and read from VTU cell arrays named Prestrain_F_g<g>, one per Gauss point.
